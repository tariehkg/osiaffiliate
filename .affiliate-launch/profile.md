# Niche Profile — OSI Affiliate

## Positioning
- **Category:** A curated B2B software directory — small businesses and solopreneurs choosing the tools to run and grow their business
- **Target Buyer:** A small business owner, solopreneur, or in-house marketer who needs to pick a CRM, email platform, website builder, project management tool, or SEO tool, and doesn't want to wade through 1,600+ vendor listings to do it
- **Primary Action:** Click through to a recommended tool's own signup/pricing page via an affiliate link
- **Real Differentiator:** A short, opinionated shortlist per category instead of an open marketplace — and an explicit, prominent explanation of how the site makes money, in deliberate contrast to how quietly Capterra/G2 disclose their own paid-placement model
- **Positioning Statement:** OSI Affiliate is a software directory for people who want a straight answer, not a 1,600-listing wall. Every category gets a handful of real picks, each backed by a real comparison, with the business model stated plainly instead of buried.

## Monetization & Trust
- **Affiliate Network(s):** Per-vendor affiliate/referral programs, joined individually as each software company is added (not yet finalized which network per vendor — flexible by design; many of the target vendors run their own programs — e.g. HubSpot, GetResponse, Systeme.io, Notion)
- **How Picks Are Chosen:** Researched — real, current features, pricing, and known affiliate-program terms compared side by side. Not yet hands-on tested; the site says so plainly rather than implying otherwise.
- **Disclosure Requirement:** Visible on every page carrying affiliate links — and treated as a trust-building headline feature (per the niche scan's "Gap" finding), not a buried footer line
- **Proof on Hand:** None yet — a new site. Do not fabricate review counts, ratings, or testimonials. Trust is built through comparison depth and disclosure transparency instead.

## Voice & Tone
- **Personality:** Straightforward, curatorial, quietly confident
- **Register:** Talks like someone who actually compared the tools, not a marketing page for any one of them
- **Sounds like:** "Here's what we'd actually pick, and why," plain pricing and feature comparisons, honest tradeoffs
- **Never sounds like:** Generic SaaS marketing copy, vendor press-release language, inflated urgency ("Limited time!"), implying hands-on testing that hasn't happened

### Visual Style
- **Keywords:** Curated, direct, considered, trustworthy, unhurried
- **What competitors are doing:** Star ratings + review counts on every card, AI-summarized sentiment blurbs, pros/cons vote counts, "Sponsored" labels mixed quietly into ranked lists, minimal disclosure — see `competitor-scan.md`
- **Do:** Make the shortlist feel deliberately short and considered; make disclosure a visible, confident statement; use real product names, pricing, and features throughout
- **Don't:** Imitate the "open marketplace with 1,600 listings" feel; imply hands-on testing that hasn't happened; fabricate review counts or star ratings; bury the affiliate disclosure
