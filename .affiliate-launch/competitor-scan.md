# Niche Scan — B2B Software Directory / Review Site

## Sites Studied
- **Capterra.com** — Gartner-owned (now G2-owned as of Jan 2026), ~80+ business software categories, monetizes via vendor pay-per-click lead generation ($2/click, $500/mo vendor minimum), not consumer affiliate commissions.
- **G2.com** — Acquired Capterra, Software Advice, and GetApp from Gartner for $110M in Jan 2026, consolidating the four largest B2B software review platforms into one company. 3.6M+ reviews, monetizes via vendor subscriptions, paid category placements, and PPC.
- **Software Advice** — Concierge-style: free one-on-one advisor consultations to narrow software choices, instead of pure self-serve browsing.
- **GetApp** — Similar model, leans SMB/SaaS-focused rather than enterprise.
- **TrustRadius** — Positioned on review-quality integrity (48% of submitted reviews rejected for quality).

## Common Category Structure
Every major player organizes by **business function**, not by price or company size: CRM, Project Management, Accounting & Finance, HR, Marketing/Marketing Automation, Sales, Customer Service, E-Commerce, Collaboration & Productivity, IT Management, and dozens more verticals (Healthcare, Legal, Construction, Hospitality). Categories run into the hundreds to thousands at the G2/Capterra scale — this breadth took over a decade and a $110M acquisition to assemble; a launch site cannot and should not attempt to match it.

## Page Types in Use
- **Category hub** — "Best [Category] Software" — ranked/filterable list, the core page type on every platform
- **Sub-views within a hub** — Trending / Highest Rated / Easiest to Use / Free (G2's pattern) — same category, different sort lenses
- **Individual product profile** — dedicated page per software product
- **Comparison tool** — side-by-side, usually 3-4 products, attribute-by-attribute
- **AI-generated FAQ block** — bottom of every category page, clearly aimed at capturing long-tail search queries
- **Buying-guide / "how to choose" content** — Capterra's "5 Key Steps for Finding the Right Software"

## Proof & Trust Patterns
- Star rating + review count on every single product card, no exceptions (e.g. "4.4/5 (13,933)")
- Short "What do users say?" AI-summarized sentiment blurb per product
- Pros/Cons mini-breakdown with vote counts (e.g. "Ease of Use (1216)")
- Site-wide trust stats near the top of the homepage ("2.5M+ Verified Reviews," "50M+ Users Yearly")
- Badges: "AI Verified," "All-in-One" — earned/algorithmic, not sold

## Disclosure Norms
Both platforms' actual monetization (vendor-paid placement and leads) is **not disclosed anywhere near the product cards** — "Sponsored" appears as a small in-line label on paid listings mixed into the organic G2 ranked list, and that's essentially the extent of it. This is a real gap, not a pattern to copy: a site monetizing through individual affiliate links has an FTC disclosure obligation these giants mostly sidestep by calling it "advertising" rather than "affiliate" relationships.

## Buyer Language
Recurring phrasing across both platforms: "Best [X] Software," "[X] for small business," "easiest to use," "free [X] software," "[X] with [specific feature]." Category pages are explicitly built to catch someone typing "best crm software" or "best project management software for small teams" into a search engine — informational-to-transactional intent, not top-of-funnel awareness content.

## The Gap
Three real openings for a new, independent, affiliate-monetized entrant:
1. **Disclosure and independence as a selling point.** Neither Capterra nor G2 is an "affiliate" in the classic sense — they're paid-placement lead-gen platforms with sponsored results mixed into "organic" rankings, disclosed minimally. A site that's explicit about "here's how we make money, and it never changes the ranking" is a genuine differentiator, not just a compliance checkbox.
2. **Depth over breadth.** Trying to cover 80-2,000 categories on day one is not a real plan for a new site — it's what a decade and a $110M acquisition bought G2. A focused set of categories, covered well, with real comparison depth, is the only credible starting point.
3. **A curated, opinionated shortlist instead of an open marketplace.** G2/Capterra list every vendor who signs up (1,658 CRM listings alone) — that's overwhelming, not helpful. A site that says "here are the 5-6 tools actually worth your time in this category" serves the buyer better and is a much more natural fit for a small, curated affiliate directory than trying to be an open catalog.
