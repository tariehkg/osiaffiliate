We checked the current pricing and feature pages for these seven on October 18, 2026, and focused on the limits that change a buying decision.

Here is our short answer:

- **General service business with bookings or payments:** Wix.
- **Portfolio, brand or polished service site:** Squarespace.
- **Store where orders and inventory are the business:** Shopify.
- **Custom-designed site or structured content:** Webflow.
- **Managed publishing and plugin flexibility without self-hosting:** WordPress.com.
- **Low allocated monthly cost, with a four-year prepayment to read first:** Hostinger.
- **Course, offer or email-led sales path:** Systeme.io.

Every pick below also names a reason to skip it. The quickest route through this page is the first table, then read closely for the two you're torn between.

## What actually decides the choice

Five things matter more than template counts, and they tend to hide until after launch.

**Maintenance ownership.** Who edits the site next month? A managed builder such as Squarespace, Wix or WordPress.com handles hosting, security and updates for you. Webflow expects someone on your side who understands layout. Hostinger is managed hosting, but its cheapest route asks you to accept a plan structure rather than a simple month-to-month.

**What "free" costs you.** Wix's free tier is a build environment with Wix branding and a Wix subdomain. Squarespace has no free plan at all. Systeme.io's free plan is real enough to take a payment, but it is capped in ways that matter: one automation rule, one workflow, three funnels, one course and 500 students. Free plans let you check whether their limits fit your launch.

**The real totals.** An advertised monthly price can be an annual-equivalent paid in full, a multi-year prepayment, or a first-year-only teaser. Domain vouchers, mailboxes, card processing, store-platform fees and renewal jumps change the number in ways a logo-rich pricing page can bury.

**What you can leave with.** "Own your content" is not the same as "export your site." Squarespace and WordPress.com can export content, but that will not reassemble your exact theme, plugins and behavior. Webflow can export code only with a paid Workspace, and its dynamic CMS and forms are not ready for self-hosted static use. Before you rely on any export promise, test the specific fields and functions you care about. Every move is at least partly a rebuild.

**SEO controls are not SEO outcomes.** Metadata, canonicals, sitemaps and redirects help you do the work, but none of these guarantee rankings. Treat them as required controls, not a promise.

## Wix: the general service-site default

<!-- media:wix -->

Wix is the pick when the site has to carry a real business action: taking a booking or a payment without duct-taping four subscriptions together. The free plan does not give you a custom-domained business launch; it shows Wix branding and keeps your address on a Wix subdomain.

The current US-reference annual-equivalents move quickly. Light is $17/month equivalent with 2 GB and two collaborators. Core is $29 with 50 GB and five collaborators, plus payments, basic e-commerce and scheduling. That Core tier is the relevant starting route for a local service that needs booking or a payment, because those tools are already included. Business at $39 adds more marketing capacity and collaborators, but you should not assume every local service needs it. These are annual-equivalents, but [Wix's pricing page](https://www.wix.com/plans) states it bills yearly subscriptions in full at purchase, and regional prices plus tax change the checkout number.

**Skip Wix if** your job is a large multi-channel inventory operation; Shopify is the specialist for that, and the documented inventory, shipping and order tooling is the reason to compare it. A portfolio-heavy brand may also prefer Squarespace's template flow.

**Watch the extras.** The one-year domain voucher is for a new domain and cannot renew an existing one. A business email address or mailbox is a separate purchase, and apps from the Wix market can add recurring cost.

**Next check:** open the checkout for your region and compare the Core and Business totals, then follow one booking or payment through to the confirmation step before paying.

## Squarespace: the portfolio and service-brand pick

Squarespace is the choice when the site itself is the brand: a photographer, consultant, architect, small practice or creative whose presentation carries part of the trust. It has no free plan. Basic is $25/month on monthly billing or $19/month annual-equivalent; Core is $39 monthly or $29 annual-equivalent, as shown on the [Squarespace pricing page](https://www.squarespace.com/pricing).

The pricing shape matters if you intend to sell. Basic allows two contributors and carries a 2% online-store transaction fee. Core removes that platform fee and lifts the contributor cap, but it does not remove card processing. Basic card rates start around 2.9% plus $0.30, and the details vary by plan and payment method. Digital-content and membership fees are a separate calculation again. The free domain is an annual-plan first-year eligibility, and business email or scheduling tools such as Acuity or Squarespace Email Campaigns can be additional costs rather than silently included.

**Choose Squarespace over Wix** when your site is primarily a brand presentation and you want the template flow and consistent sitewide styling. Choose Wix over Squarespace when bookings, payments and a managed local-service workflow are the primary job and you want those tools tightly bundled.

**Skip Squarespace if** you need a large product catalog or a free plan. It can sell, but commerce is not the reason to buy it.

**Next check:** add up platform fee plus your actual card rate on a sample order. Then decide whether Basic or Core is honest for your transaction volume.

## Shopify: when products are the business

Shopify is built around store operations rather than pages. Variants, inventory, locations, collections, shipping and order management are the center of the product, and the current [Shopify pricing page](https://www.shopify.com/pricing) defaulted to the annual view: Basic at $29/month equivalent, Grow at $79, Advanced at $299.

Basic is enough to sell unlimited products, run casual in-person sales and use the core checkout. Grow adds staff accounts and lower card rates. Advanced adds live third-party shipping rates and more control. The plan is not the only cost. Card processing starts near 2.9% plus 30¢ for Basic, and choosing a third-party payment provider can add a separate transaction fee. Paid themes and apps are additional recurring or one-time costs, and a store grows into them quickly.

**Skip Shopify if** you are a service business, coach, creative or content-first site. The non-commerce pages are not the product, and you would be paying for inventory tooling you will barely touch.

**Migration reality.** Moving a mature Shopify store is not a clean one-click export. Products and customers may export, but checkout customizations, scripts and app logic generally require rebuilding in the new tool.

**Next check:** before choosing a theme, estimate the apps and theme cost, and verify your payment-provider route, because that can change the next monthly bill more than the plan tier.

## Webflow: custom design and structured content

<!-- media:webflow -->

Webflow is the list's specialist for design control and structured content. It works with real layout concepts—divs, classes, flex, grids, breakpoints—so a designer can produce a site that matches a specific vision without hand-writing the whole front end. The underrated part is the CMS: collections with fields, useful for blogs, case studies, job boards or catalogs that must stay consistent as they grow.

Clear up the pricing before you get lost in it. Webflow separates Site plans from Workspace plans. For a CMS-powered site, **Premium is the documented Site-plan starting point** at $25/month billed yearly, or $39/month on monthly billing, with a default of 50 GB bandwidth, as listed on the [Webflow pricing page](https://webflow.com/pricing). You do **not** need the paid Core Workspace just to add custom code to a paid site; custom code is on the paid Site plan itself. Core, at $19/month billed yearly, matters mainly for code export or extra staging and workspace needs. Bandwidth beyond the default is sold in configured add-on bundles, not a flat per-gigabyte rate.

**Skip Webflow if** nobody on your side will learn it. It is precise, but it assumes you understand page layout, and that learning curve is real. You can read our [full Webflow review](/webflow-review) for a closer look before you commit.

**Export honesty.** Webflow's code export requires a paid Workspace, and even then CMS items and forms are not drop-in ready for a self-hosted static host. Do not plan your exit strategy around a clean, complete export.

**Next check:** decide before building whether code export is part of your future, because that determines whether the paid Workspace is part of the budget.

## WordPress.com: managed WordPress publishing

WordPress.com is the managed version of WordPress. That distinction matters: WordPress.com hosts and maintains the installation, while WordPress.org is open-source software you install on a host you buy and manage yourself. For most small-business readers who want WordPress without the update-and-security workload, WordPress.com is the answer.

Personal is the plan worth noticing. At $4/month annual-equivalent on the [WordPress.com pricing page](https://wordpress.com/pricing/), the current page explicitly includes the ability to install plugins, along with 6 GB of storage. You no longer need the old Business-tier story to use plugins. Premium is $8/month annual-equivalent and adds storage and a richer feature set; Business, at $25, adds developer tools and real-time backups.

**Pick WordPress.com** for serious publishing, a blog-centered business, or when you specifically want the WordPress plugin ecosystem with managed hosting. The block editor is closer to WordPress publishing than a freeform visual canvas, which is exactly where the builder's intensity reveals itself.

**Portability caution.** You can export content, but content export does not guarantee the same theme, plugin behavior and layout will appear on a new host. A move is a rebuild, not a clone.

**Next check:** verify the storage and plan boundary on the exact billing cycle you would use, because the promotional monthly numbers are often multi-year annual-equivalents.

## Hostinger: low allocated cost, with a four-year prepayment

On the [Hostinger website-builder pricing page](https://www.hostinger.com/website-builder), Premium is displayed at $2.99/month. The fine print is explicit: that requires 48 months, so the cash due today is **$143.52**. Allocated across the first year, that is about $35.88. After the term, it renews at $10.99/month. Unlimited is displayed at $3.99/month, which is **$191.52** up front for 48 months, renewing at $16.99. Cloud Startup, for heavier use, is $7.99/month displayed and $383.52 up front for 48 months.

Premium covers up to three websites, a first-year custom domain and two mailboxes per website for the first year. That is a real combination for a simple portfolio or business showcase. E-commerce is not on the entry Premium line; store features appear from Unlimited or Cloud Startup upward.

**Choose Hostinger** when you can accept a four-year commitment and want a low allocated monthly cost over that term. **Skip it** when you need predictable month-to-month billing, a serious online store. Note that WordPress.com Personal at $48 per year billed annually is a lower cash outlay for a single managed publishing site, so "cheap" depends on what you are buying.

**Two renewals to write down:** the plan rate after the discount term, and the domain/mailbox renewal dates after their first-year inclusions expire.

## Systeme.io: the funnel-and-course path

Systeme.io is a different species from the rest of this list. Funnels, email campaigns, automation, courses, communities, bookings and a simple CRM sit in one account. It is the pick for a solo coach, course creator or info-product seller whose website is really a path from landing page to payment to follow-up.

The free plan is both generous and easy to misread. It is permanent and needs no credit card, and it includes 2,000 contacts, three funnels, one automation rule, one workflow, one course with 500 students, one custom domain, unlimited email sends and 0% platform transaction fees, as shown on the <a href="https://systeme.io/pricing?sa=sa0038519698989c6bdc2b4b264f6b3241bacb1095" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">Systeme.io pricing page</a>. Those caps are the issue: one automation rule and one workflow may be enough for a welcome sequence, but not necessarily for every follow-up branch you have planned. At $17/month, Startup raises the room to 5,000 contacts, 10 funnels, 5 courses, 3 custom domains and more automation. Webinar and Unlimited go up from there. See our [Systeme.io pricing breakdown](/systeme-io-pricing) for the full ladder, and our [Systeme.io review](/systeme-io-review) if this is your shortlist.

**Choose Systeme.io** when the offer and email path is the business. **Skip it** when the website is your brand. Its page design is built around conversion, not bespoke visual control, and it will frustrate a design-led reader.

**Migration realism.** If you build your operational brain here, count the switching cost before you grow dependent.

**Next check:** map your welcome sequence and buyer follow-up against the free plan's one automation rule and one workflow. One workflow can hold multiple actions and branches, so count the actual separate automations you need before assuming you need Startup.

## The decision flow

<!-- media:flow -->

Run these five questions before you let a template gallery decide for you.

1. **Is this a store, a brand, or a path to sale?** Products as the business points to Shopify. A portfolio or service brand points to Squarespace. A landing-page-to-payment-to-email path points to Systeme.io.
2. **Does design need to be custom, or does it need to be consistent?** Webflow buys you control and assumes someone will learn it. The managed builders buy you consistency and speed of maintenance.
3. **Do you want the WordPress plugin ecosystem, or a visual builder?** WordPress.com is the low-maintenance route to WordPress publishing; Wix and Squarespace are the visual-builder route.
4. **Is your constraint cash today or predictable monthly?** Hostinger spreads cost over a four-year prepay but asks for the full amount now. WordPress.com Personal is $48 for one year, and Systeme.io Free starts at zero. Read the billing shape, not just the headline number.
5. **Who maintains this in year two?** If you cannot name that person, choose a builder you can maintain yourself after launch.

Once you answer those, there is usually one clear pick and one backup. For more of this decision language, see the [website-builders category](/website-builders).

## The pilot checklist before you pay

Before paying for a plan, run the same checks in a free tier or trial:

- Build with your **real content**, not demo text.
- Create the **core item** you will use most: one product, one service/booking, one post, or one landing page plus thank-you page.
- Note every **included-versus-paid boundary**: domain, mailbox, scheduling, email sends, automation rules, contributors, store fees.
- Try an **export or copy** of your sample content and record what does not come across.
- Write down every **renewal rate** and first-year-only inclusion.
- Name the **person who will edit pages** next month, and have them perform one edit.

Use the [website-builder evaluation sheet](/downloads/website-builder-evaluation.csv) as a blank scorecard for the two finalists, and keep the fields honest rather than filling them during a sales demo. These are synthetic, pre-built fields for you to fill in — not a report of anyone's finished test on your behalf.

If you want the pure numbers before reading product pages, work through the [website-builder cost calculator](/website-builder-cost-calculator). It gives you a first-year and renewal view, shows the billing inputs, and keeps your figures in the browser.

## Choose one, then verify the tripwire

You should be able to walk away from this page with a single leading pick and a reason to protect against it. Start with the free tier or trial for that builder, complete the pilot checklist on real content, and confirm the renewal rates before paying. If you are between a general business site and an offer-led path, Systeme.io's free plan is a free way to test whether a funnel is what you actually run. If you are between a brand site and a store, price the real card and platform fees on one order before choosing Squarespace or Shopify.

The one action that prevents a rebuild later is to prove the **most important workflow** works on the plan you intend to buy, not on an unlabeled demo.
