The three picks below are the permanent-free kind, not trials, and each one stops somewhere. The stopping point is the actual decision.

If you're a solo operator who can live with sending invoices outside the app, **FieldVibe Solo** is a free starting point: unlimited jobs and clients, one device, and semi-automated reminders that come from your own phone rather than a dedicated number. If you have a small crew and the job is scheduling, time clock, forms, and crew communication rather than customer invoicing, **Connecteam's Small Business plan** is a free workforce option, with one quiet catch: archived users still count toward your ten. If you want a small team moving from scheduled work into an invoicing workflow on a free plan, **Dusk FSM Launch** is the interesting middle option, with a 25-completed-Work-items monthly cap and a quote-and-invoice area we could not fully verify from public documentation.

The common thread: every permanent-free plan here has a hard boundary somewhere in the job-to-payment loop. The decision is whether the boundary sits in a place you can work around.

## Separate "free plan" from "trial" before you build anything

A 14-day trial is not a free plan. That distinction sounds obvious, but it disappears in a lot of roundups because the trial and the paid product look the same for two weeks.

The clearest paid-side examples we checked while comparing boundaries:

- **[Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/)** offers a 14-day trial on its Grow plan with no credit card, then it becomes a paid subscription you continue by entering billing information. The entry Core plan for one user is $49/month with no commitment, $39/month with a 12-month commitment billed monthly, or a $29/month annual equivalent. That annual equivalent works out to roughly $348 as an estimated prepaid year; the no-commitment route is about $588 a year at $49/month. We found no permanent-free Jobber route in the pricing materials we captured.
- **[Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/)** offers a 14-day free trial with no card, then pricing is generated from a tailored quote based on your industry, team size, and goals. The public pricing page mentioned a "from $59" figure in its title, but we could not tie that number to a specific plan, team size, and billing cadence, so we treat an eligible Housecall Pro quote as unknown until you receive one.

The three picks below are a different deal: no trial clock. Their limits are structural, and they're visible before you enter a card.

Two patterns jump out. First, FieldVibe's free tier is expressly solo — one user, one device — and it deliberately stops before quotes and invoices. Connecteam and Dusk can also cover a solo operator for free, but their limits are structured around team size and Work volume rather than device count. Second, Connecteam and Dusk both allow teams for free, but they split the field service job differently: Connecteam is workforce coordination without customer invoicing; Dusk points at an invoicing workflow without verified quote-to-accounting parity.

## FieldVibe Solo: the free solo start that stops before you bill

FieldVibe's [Solo plan](https://www.fieldvibe.com/pricing/) is permanently free for one user, and it's aimed at a specific reader: an owner-operator running jobs alone. The free plan includes unlimited jobs and clients, one-time and recurring visit scheduling, manual and semi-automated reminders sent from your own phone, a business website to accept work requests, a services and products price list, client history and notes, and a way to collect customer reviews.

The catch is the billing boundary. Quotes, invoices, online payments, time tracking, revenue tracking, tasks, journaling, reports, and fully automated reminders from a dedicated number require **Solo Pro at $20/month**. That plan also unlocks multiple devices. If you add a team, **Crews** is $50/month with unlimited staff, custom roles and permissions, and a higher reminder allowance (1,000 automated reminders per month for US and Canada, 400 globally, versus Solo Pro's 400 in the US and Canada and 150 globally).

There's also a gap we could not close from the public pages: we did not find a documented QuickBooks integration on the inspected FieldVibe pricing and feature material. That's a verification item for your pilot, not proof the connection doesn't exist.

**Choose FieldVibe Solo if** you work alone, you already invoice through another route you're happy with, and you mainly want to stop carrying the schedule and customer details in your head.

**Skip it if** you need in-app quotes and invoices without paying, you need more than one device, or you plan to add even one crew member soon. Within FieldVibe, the jump from Solo to Solo Pro is small and mostly about billing capability; the jump to Crews is a larger operational change.

## Connecteam Small Business: strong free workforce management, but no customer invoicing

Connecteam's [Small Business plan](https://connecteam.com/small-business-plan/) is the most unusual free offer we looked at because the vendor says it includes access to all hubs and most advanced features for up to 10 users, at Expert-plan capability. What it actually covers is workforce operations: a GPS time clock, scheduling, quick tasks, checklists and forms with photos, signatures and GPS stamps, chat, an updates feed, a directory, knowledge base, and a wide set of HR and training tools.

Two conditions change the decision before you sign up. First, the 10-user allowance counts **active and archived users**. If you've archived former employees for payroll or compliance reasons, they can still occupy a seat. Second, Connecteam is not a customer receivables system. Its own category roundup acknowledges the lack of quoting and invoicing, and those are the pieces we verified. Any separate payment handling remained unverified in the pages we captured. That means Connecteam coordinates the people doing the work, but the customer's quote, invoice, and payment collection sit outside the plan.

Above 10 users, the free path narrows. Connecteam's [pricing page](https://connecteam.com/pricing/) documents a **Limited plan that's free for up to 30 users** with reduced essentials per hub after the 14-day trial. It is genuinely free, but it is not the same feature set as the Small Business plan, so the exact functions you lose need to be checked against your workflows before you roll past ten users. Paid hubs then start at $35/month for the first 30 users on monthly billing for the Operations Hub Basic tier, with a displayed $29/month annual equivalent. Buying Basic above 10 users can mean losing some of the Expert-level functions the free plan included, so the Limited fallback is worth comparing before any paid purchase.

**Choose Connecteam if** you have two to ten field employees and your problem is scheduling, time capture for payroll, forms and checklists in the field, and keeping the crew in one chat instead of a group text.

**Skip it if** your day revolves around quoting, invoicing, and payment collection inside one system, or if you're poised to pass 10 users and would rather avoid a tier transition that changes your feature set. A crew that needs customer billing will still need a separate quoting or accounting product, which is fine if you planned for it and expensive if you didn't.

## Dusk FSM Launch: real work-to-invoice at a 25-Work-item ceiling

Dusk's [Launch plan](https://duskmobile.com/free-field-service-management-software/) is a free team option with an invoicing workflow. It includes up to 25 completed Work items per month, unlimited staff and contractors, no credit card, scheduling and dispatch, forms, time tracking, mobile capture of notes, photos and signatures, an invoicing workflow, and unlimited integration connectors including QuickBooks Online.

The 25-completed-Work-items cap is more useful than an unqualified "unlimited staff" claim because it defines the actual free boundary. Beyond 25, you upgrade to Essentials or Growth; there's no overage billing on Launch. The official page says additional completed Work items on paid plans are billed at $3 or $4 per Work item depending on the plan, but we could not verify a base paid subscription rate from the public page. That per-item rate is one allowance detail, not a total price, so treat paid Dusk pricing as a quote to get.

Two things stay unverified, and both matter for accounting. "Unlimited connectors" is not the same as unlimited transaction capacity or field-by-field parity with QuickBooks Online. And the captured page documents an **invoicing workflow** — moving completed work toward invoicing — but it does not establish the entire customer quoting-and-invoicing process, editing behavior after an invoice is touched in accounting, or tax treatment. We also couldn't confirm how Dusk defines a completed Work item when jobs are returned, reopened, or recurring, so your first month of real use is partly a measurement exercise.

**Choose Dusk Launch if** you have a small team that wants to stop running jobs through spreadsheets and start connecting work to invoices at low volume, and you're willing to verify the accounting parity yourself.

**Skip it if** a normal month already exceeds 25 completed Work items, or if confirmed quote and invoice field parity with your bookkeeping is a dealbreaker before you start. Its billing path still has unresolved details.

## What free actually costs once you're running

Free software subscriptions are $0, but a $0 subscription is not a zero-cost operation. The useful formula is:

**First-year total = 12 × (software monthly + extras monthly + external tools monthly + processing monthly) + one-time setup**

Then track a **labor scenario separately**, because it's a judgment call, not a subscription: your hourly rate multiplied by the time you spend re-entering data, chasing reminders, or duplicating records between tools.

<!-- media:flow -->

Worked examples from the formula:

- A solo cleaner on FieldVibe Solo enters $0 for software. If they want in-app quotes and invoices, the software line becomes $20/month, or about $240 over a year on monthly billing. If they stay free and invoice through a separate tool, that external tool's monthly cost and any card-processing fees are each multiplied by 12 and added to the first-year total.
- A six-person landscaping crew on Connecteam Small Business enters $0 for software, but nothing in the plan creates customer invoices, so external accounting or invoicing is a required line item, not an option. If that separate tool runs $30/month, it adds roughly $360 to the first-year estimate before processing fees. Payment processing fees apply wherever cards are accepted.
- A Dusk Launch user at 20 completed Work items enters $0. At 30 completed items, Launch no longer fits; the paid base rate is an unknown quote, and the public page's $3 or $4 per additional Work item is only part of the paid picture. That's a boundary, not a simple monthly number, and you should not project a 30-item bill from the per-item line alone.

The labor scenario is where free plans can quietly cost money. If you spend 40 minutes a week copying job details into an invoicing or accounting tool because your free plan doesn't bill, that's about 34.7 hours a year. At $30/hour, that's roughly $1,040. That's a hypothetical you can adjust to your actual minutes and rate — it's not a measured saving or a guarantee — but it shows why "free software" and "free operation" aren't the same number.

## Run a pilot before you commit

The fastest way to find a boundary you can't live with is to push synthetic data through the actual free plan for a week, not to read another feature list. Keep the pilot isolated from live customer data and from your real accounting file, especially if you're testing an integration route.

Use the [home-service evaluation scorecard](/downloads/home-service-evaluation.csv) first. It's a blank requirements sheet: list the jobs, quotes, invoices, payments, and accounting connections your current week actually needs, then mark each pick as confirmed, unconfirmed, or doesn't fit. The observation cells stay blank until you observe them; resist filling them from the vendor's marketing page.

Then run the [home-service pilot worksheet](/downloads/home-service-pilot.csv), which is a synthetic request-to-visit-to-invoice checklist. The idea is to follow one fictional job through each product: create the job, schedule it, complete the work, attach a photo or signature where the plan allows, and attempt the quote or invoice step. For Dusk, specifically record how a completed Work item behaves when reopened or marked complete a second time, and whether an invoice edited in your accounting tool stays consistent when you look back at Dusk.

For any QuickBooks path, the safest check is an accountant-supervised test in an isolated or synthetic accounting environment with a backup, not a month of double postings in your live books. Mark the record-level results only after they happen; a blank observation is an honest result.

## When free stops fitting

Free plans end, and the boundary tells you which paid direction is least painful.

If you're outgrowing FieldVibe's single-user or separate-billing shape, Solo Pro at $20/month adds billing functions, and Crews at $50/month is the team-grade step inside the same product. If you need a fuller quote-to-invoice-to-payment loop from day one, the paid category leaders are worth pricing directly: Jobber's Core starts at $49/month with no commitment for one user, and Housecall Pro requires a tailored quote after its trial. We detail those products in our [best field service management software for small business roundup](/best-field-service-management-software-small-business), and our [Jobber review](/jobber-review) covers what the entry plan includes and where its feature gates sit.

If your reason for staying free is really "I don't want to commit before I prove the workflow," the pilot above matters more than the pick. A free plan that recreates manual billing work can cost more in year one than a paid plan that collects the invoice in the same screen where the job closed.

## Stay-put and migration checks

If you're already paying for Jobber, Housecall Pro, or another full FSM product, a free plan can look like savings. Check three things before switching:

1. Does the free boundary cover your real completed-job volume? Dusk's 25-Work-item cap and FieldVibe's one-user, one-device limit are hard edges, not suggestions.
2. Does the free product keep invoicing and payments inside your workflow, or does it hand them back to a spreadsheet and a second tool? The labor scenario above is the honest way to price that handoff.
3. Have you tested export and accounting parity before moving records? Run the pilot with synthetic data first.

Our [field service software and QuickBooks guide](/field-service-software-quickbooks) walks through the connector questions by edition and direction where the vendors publish them, including the difference between a legacy two-way sync and a newer one-way route.

## Next steps by situation

- **You're a solo operator who invoices outside the app:** Start FieldVibe Solo, complete the evaluation scorecard against your actual weekly workflow without copying any live customer records into the software, and decide after a week whether $20/month for Solo Pro beats your current separate invoicing step.
- **You have a crew and invoicing isn't your bottleneck:** Start Connecteam Small Business, count your active *and* archived users before inviting anyone, and confirm whether an 11th user would move you to the free Limited plan or a paid hub, along with which features change.
- **You have a small team and want work to flow toward invoices for free:** Confirm your normal monthly volume stays at or below 25 completed Work items before starting Dusk Launch. If you already exceed that, request the paid quote first and run the work-to-invoice pilot before trusting the accounting path.
- **You're already outgrowing free:** Skip the free tier entirely and compare the paid shortlist in our [home services software guides](/home-services-software), then price Jobber and a Housecall Pro quote against your actual monthly job count.
