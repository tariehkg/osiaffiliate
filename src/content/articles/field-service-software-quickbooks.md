Before you spend an afternoon on demos, check three things against the exact QuickBooks edition you already pay for: which records sync, which direction they go, and whether the behavior you’re reading about applies to a new account or a legacy one. If you’re on QuickBooks Online and want transparent pricing with a documented real-time push, Jobber’s Connect tier is the clearest starting point for a small team — as long as you accept that the ongoing sync flows out toward QuickBooks, not back. If QuickBooks Desktop is non-negotiable, Housecall Pro is the better documented fit, and QuickBooks itself is a separate bill. ServiceTitan supports both editions for larger multi-trade shops, but you have to ask what your quoted package actually passes. A genuinely free start exists in Dusk FSM’s Launch plan, but its QuickBooks field-level details are something we could not verify from public documentation.

## The three checks that settle most of it

You can narrow the field quickly by answering three questions.

**Which edition?** Some tools maintain an ongoing sync only with QuickBooks Online. Others document QuickBooks Desktop support too. If you run Desktop on purpose — plenty of trade shops do — a tool that only reaches QuickBooks Online forces you to change your accounting stack or manually export data, which defeats the purchase.

**Which direction?** An outward, one-way push gets invoices and payments into QuickBooks but doesn’t bring your bookkeeper’s edits back into the field tool. A change to a billing address in QuickBooks won’t update dispatch. That’s fine if you accept QuickBooks as the final bookkeeping authority and your field tool as the operational source. It is not the same as two-way integration, and headlines rarely tell you which you’re getting.

**Which records?** “Integrates” can mean only invoices. For real job costing or payroll you also need items, payments, timesheets, and expenses to arrive with the right mapping — income account, tax status, employee match. The more records you push, the more setup decisions and exceptions you own before it’s dependable.

Keep those checks beside you. They decide the table below.

## QuickBooks Online: Jobber’s new sync is one-way

Jobber’s older QuickBooks integration was manually triggered and let clients and items move both directions. [Jobber’s help article](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/how-items-sync-between-jobber-and-quickbooks-online/) is explicit that this is the *legacy* version, and that first-time connections now use a new integration. The page’s migration banner says any unsynced Jobber changes push to QuickBooks during the move, then the ongoing new sync becomes **one-way from Jobber to QuickBooks in real time** — and once you move, you cannot go back.

That is a meaningful, current scope change. Do not read an older review or forum post describing two-way Jobber behavior and assume it still describes a new account. It doesn’t.

The second Jobber gate is which plan you buy. Jobber Core — $49 per month for one user with no commitment — does not include QuickBooks Online. QBO first appears in Connect, at $139 per month for one user or $199 for five with no commitment, according to [Jobber’s public pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) captured on October 18, 2026. The boundary is the billing. Housecall Pro’s own FAQ says QuickBooks is not included in your Housecall Pro subscription. You pay for both. ## What the subscription doesn’t cover

You are pricing at least three bills, not one.

**The field service subscription.** Core: $49/month one user no commitment; Connect: $139 one user or $199 five users no commitment; Grow: $199 one user or $299 five users no commitment. Annual equivalents lower the monthly number but are prepaid estimates — for Connect at one user, the displayed $99 monthly equivalent becomes an estimated $1,188 per year, not an exact invoice total.

**QuickBooks itself.** Housecall Pro states this outright, and the same applies across the routes in this article: the accounting platform is a separate subscription. Sync software does not replace your QuickBooks bill.

**Payment processing.** Where the field service tool collects card or bank payments, processing fees apply on top of the plan. Treat the software subscription as the floor, not the total.

Here’s a bounded labor check. If hooking up QuickBooks would replace about 40 minutes a week of manual re-entry at a $30-per-hour bookkeeping rate, that’s roughly $1,040 per year in labor. That number is smaller than Jobber Connect’s $1,668 first-year total at one user on no commitment. The calculation does not include error correction or delayed invoices — those are real possibilities, but they’re yours to estimate. Run your own figures. The point is that “sync saves money” has a break-even you can actually calculate, and for very light volume the subscription may cost more than the labor it replaces.

## Cutover: moving from double-entry to sync

Moving from manual entry or another tool to a synced setup can go wrong in a few predictable places. The sequence below forces those mapping decisions before they surface at month end.

1. **Pick one source of truth per record.** Usually that’s the field tool for jobs, invoices, and payments; QuickBooks for final bookkeeping edits. Write it down.
2. **Clean the customer list first.** Normalize names, addresses, and duplicates on both sides before the first sync. One “Jones & Sons” versus “Jones and Sons” mismatch is enough to fork a customer record.
3. **Map accounts and tax before pushing anything.** Income accounts, sales tax items, and payment accounts need destinations in QuickBooks. Do this with a chart of accounts open, not blind.
4. **Run one labeled synthetic record in an isolated test or sandbox with a backup.** Create a customer, an item, an invoice, mark it sent, sync, and inspect QuickBooks. Then record a payment and check where it lands — applied to the invoice, or parked in undeposited funds.
5. **Keep your old workflow alive until reconciliation comes back clean on the synthetic record.** Don’t post duplicate live data into QuickBooks; run the pilot against a test copy or under your accountant’s supervision.
6. **Assign an owner to the sync-status habit.** Someone needs to check on a schedule you’ll actually keep, not when the accountant asks where last month’s invoices went.

## A synthetic transaction, from visit to reconciliation

Here’s the walkthrough in miniature. It’s fictional, so use it as a shape, not a promise.

Suppose you run a two-person gutter company. A customer calls about a downspout repair. You already have her in both systems. You dispatch the job from your field tool. The tech completes the work and enters two line items: two hours of labor at $90 and $45 in materials, for a $225 invoice. The tech marks it sent, closes the visit, and takes a card payment on site.

In a working outward sync, the customer record, invoice, line items, and payment all push into QuickBooks. Your bookkeeper opens QuickBooks and sees the invoice matched to the right customer, the payment applied, and the income account correct. Nothing was re-typed.

The failure paths sit just outside that sequence, and each one is a check to run rather than an assumption to make. What happens when a tech edits the invoice after your sync runs — does QuickBooks keep the old version, or re-push? Does marking an invoice sent in your tool actually trigger the sync, or is there a separate push step? If your bookkeeper changes a billing address in QuickBooks, does the field tool’s dispatch view stay stale? Where does a payment land — applied to the invoice, or sitting in undeposited funds? If an employee’s name differs between systems, does timesheet sync fail silently or surface an error?

## When staying put is the right answer

This is not a universal upgrade. Stay put when:

- Your volume is a handful of invoices a month and your current manual workflow closes cleanly. The subscription math above may not favor you.
- Your team won’t close jobs in the mobile app. The sync can only move records your team captures in the field tool. If adoption fails, you’ve bought a new data-entry job with a nicer-looking dashboard.
- You’re on QuickBooks Desktop with a tool that already pushes invoices and you reconcile weekly without pain. The friction you have may be smaller than a migration.
- Your accounting workflow is working for the person who closes the books. The technical sync is the easy part; the human process is the contract.
