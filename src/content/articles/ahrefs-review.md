## What Ahrefs is

Ahrefs earns its slot on link data. Its index of backlinks and referring domains is the strongest of the tools here, and if your work involves digital PR, link building or working out why a competitor outranks you on authority rather than content, that is the number you live in.

## What it does well

The interface is unusually direct for the category: paste a domain, get the picture. Less time spent assembling a report is a real feature when the report is weekly.

- **Link-led growth plans.** Link-led growth plans is where Ahrefs earns its place on our SEO & Marketing Tools shortlist.
- **Digital PR and outreach.** Digital PR and outreach is where Ahrefs earns its place on our SEO & Marketing Tools shortlist.
- **SEO specialists and agencies.** SEO specialists and agencies is where Ahrefs earns its place on our SEO & Marketing Tools shortlist.

## Where it falls short

Skip it if your work is content-led and you need a free or cheap way in. There is no general free tier for researching sites you do not own, and usage limits are worth understanding before you buy.

## How Ahrefs pricing works

Tiered subscriptions from Lite upwards, with usage-based limits on how much data you can pull and extra users, projects and credits sold on top; the free Webmaster Tools tier covers only sites you own.

No general free plan; free Webmaster Tools give audit and backlink data for sites you can verify as your own.

## Ahrefs alternatives

- **[Semrush](/semrush-review)** — widest toolkit: keywords, competitors, site audits, ads. Limited free account, then paid.
- **[Moz Pro](/moz-pro-review)** — friendlier learning curve; good rank tracking. Free trial, then paid monthly.
