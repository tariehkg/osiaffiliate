## Our verdict

The honest split: *GetResponse if you are building a machine, AWeber if you are building a habit*. Both are competent senders, so the decision is about how much product you want standing between you and the send button.

There is a real cost to choosing the broader tool. Half of what GetResponse offers is page and funnel building, and if you already pay for a site builder you like, you are buying that half twice and getting the weaker version. That is the single most common reason we would steer someone to AWeber instead.

Going the other way, AWeber's simplicity is a ceiling as well as a feature. If you can already describe the automation you want — different follow-up by segment, behaviour-triggered sequences, a scoring rule — you will hit that ceiling within months. At that point neither of these is the answer and you should read our ActiveCampaign entry instead, which is on the shortlist for exactly this case.

## What this comparison does not cover

No inbox-placement testing, no send-volume benchmarks, and no numbers on either plan ladder — both vendors revise list tiers and feature gates regularly, so read their pricing pages before you commit. The judgements here come from the products, their documentation and their published feature sets rather than from running both accounts for a year. Corrections are welcome and we will change a row that is wrong.
