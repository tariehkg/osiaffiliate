For a 1,000-contact weekly newsletter with one welcome sequence, **GetResponse Starter is the lower-cost documented match at $19 per month**, with unlimited paid sends under its terms. If you need three simple workflows or phone support at that same size, **AWeber Lite or Plus may fit better**. At 500 paid contacts, the direction flips: **AWeber Lite at $15 per month undercuts GetResponse Starter’s $19 minimum contact band**.

Our separate [GetResponse review](/getresponse-review) and [AWeber review](/aweber-review) go deeper on each product alone.

Both include a list with forms and landing pages, an email builder, autoresponders and behavior-triggered workflows, broadcast split testing, payment-connected forms, import and export paths, and Zapier-style integrations. The differences sit in plan gates: workflow counts and elements, which tier unlocks behavioral automation, branding, send caps and billable-contact rules.

### Worked cases that change the cheap answer

**Basic 1,000-contact monthly newsletter.** GetResponse Starter at $19 per month is $6 less than AWeber Lite at $25, and Starter also includes unlimited paid sends and unlimited paid landing pages. That is a plan-and-capacity edge for this specific job, not a platform-wide value verdict. AWeber Lite’s paid broadcast A/B testing and phone support may justify the higher price for some buyers.

**Three small workflows at 1,000 contacts.** AWeber Lite documents 3 automations at $25 per month. GetResponse Starter documents one workflow with a six-element ceiling. If you need exactly three simple sequences and each condition is eligible on Lite, AWeber wins on count and sticker price. If you need unlimited workflows, AWeber Plus at $45 per month is $14 lower than GetResponse Marketer at $59, but those bundles are not identical.

**Creator-style business at 1,000 contacts.** GetResponse Creator starts at $69 per month and is the route we verified for included courses and a 100-attendee hosted webinar. AWeber’s inspected official route points to external webinar tools and connectors, at quoted prices we could not capture. Do not assume the lower AWeber plan price makes webinars cheaper without pricing the full external route.

Compare the plan that includes your required capability, not the cheapest plan that shares a brand name.

## Free and trial routes are not the same as paid plans

Both products keep an ongoing free route for up to 500 contacts, with different limits.

**GetResponse Free** continues after a 14-day premium period. It supports up to 500 contacts and 2,500 newsletter sends per month with one published page. After that period, **you cannot publish email automations or autoresponders, and premium scheduling is unavailable**. A free account does not prove what a paid Starter workflow can do. See the <a href="https://www.getresponse.com/help/can-i-try-getresponse-before-i-buy.html" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">GetResponse free-trial help</a>.

**AWeber Free** is a separate lifetime free account with up to 500 subscribers, 3,000 sends per month, one list and one workflow. The exact behavioral-condition gate on Free is not documented in the pages we inspected. Do not assume a Split Path branch will publish on Free without checking. See the [AWeber plan help](https://docs.aweber.com/getting-started-with-aweber/aweber-information/how-much-does-aweber-cost).

Both vendors’ paid-pricing pages advertise **14-day paid-plan trials**. A trial exposes an interface for a limited time, not the billable entitlements of a chosen subscription. A four-newsletter month at 500 contacts is 2,000 sends before welcome or other messages, which fits inside both free send caps in those months. Not every month has four issues.

## Automation and email testing: plan gates matter more than labels

**AWeber can branch**. Its current Workflows documentation includes a Split Path with Yes/No conditions based on opens, clicks and tags, with actions on both branches.

### What the tier gates actually allow

**GetResponse Starter** documents **one workflow** with a **maximum of six elements**, and excludes tags, scoring, course and ecommerce elements inside that workflow. Element count is not email count. Six elements may be enough for a short welcome sequence, but it is a tight budget. Starter can run a behavioral rule only if the rule stays within those boundaries; it does not prove every condition is unavailable. **GetResponse Marketer** moves to **unlimited workflows** but still lacks course and webinar elements. **Creator** adds those. See the <a href="https://www.getresponse.com/help/which-workflow-templates-are-available-in-my-plan.html" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">GetResponse workflow plan help</a>.

**AWeber Lite** documents **3 automations**, but the pricing card visibly marks **behavioral automation as excluded from Lite**. Treat **AWeber Plus** as the minimum documented paid plan for a required behavioral Split Path, and confirm the exact condition-to-tier mapping before purchase. **Plus** lifts the workflow cap to unlimited. AWeber also documents a **500-step per-workflow ceiling**; that is a different limit from GetResponse Starter’s six-element ceiling, and neither number measures how well a builder handles real-world logic. See the [AWeber Split Path help](https://docs.aweber.com/workflows/workflows-1/how-do-i-use-split-path-in-workflows) and [workflow steps page](https://docs.aweber.com/workflows/creating-a-workflow/workflow-steps).

### A concrete rule to reproduce before paying

Suppose you want: *a new subscriber receives a welcome email; if they click a link inside it, send a product guide; if they do not click, wait three days and send a short reintroduction.*

Both platforms can express versions of this rule, but the eligible tier differs. Reproduce the exact rule in the trial that matches your likely plan. **GetResponse Starter** may support it if the rule fits one workflow, six elements, and avoids the excluded tags, scoring, course and ecommerce elements. **GetResponse Marketer** is needed when workflow quantity, more than six elements, tagging or scoring rules Starter out. **AWeber Plus** is the route to test a behavioral Split Path; do not use Lite as that test bed. Confirm publishing conditions before assuming the draft will run on the paid plan. Both products have a branching route at an appropriate eligible plan.

### Broadcast A/B versus workflow testing

- **Broadcast A/B testing** splits a one-time campaign. GetResponse includes it on **Marketer and Creator**, not Starter. AWeber documents a two-variant test with a reserve group and manual winner selection on Lite and Plus. See the [AWeber split-test page](https://docs.aweber.com/broadcasts/split-testing-broadcasts).
- **Workflow testing** compares two entire paths. GetResponse’s percentage Splitter randomly allocates contacts and requires manual analysis. AWeber’s documented workflow-test route copies a workflow, applies a distinct trigger tag, directs contacts into versions manually or through forms, then reviews stats and selects a winner by hand. See the <a href="https://www.getresponse.com/help/how-does-the-splitter-filter-work.html" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">GetResponse Splitter page</a> and [AWeber workflow-test page](https://docs.aweber.com/workflows/workflows-1/how-do-i-split-test-my-workflows).

The pages we inspected describe manual analysis and manual winner selection. They do not describe automated statistical significance or an automatic winner. 

## Pages, creator bundle, support and the tools you already pay for

**GetResponse paid plans include unlimited landing pages**, with 3 users on Starter and 5 on Marketer and Creator. **AWeber Lite includes 3 pages, 3 users, 1 list and 1 custom segment**; Plus removes those caps. A buyer running one list and one page may not notice Lite’s caps; multiple lead magnets push toward Plus.

AWeber Lite keeps an **email badge**, and its pricing card visibly crosses out branding removal. Removal is a Plus-only gate for the email badge. Do not generalize that gate to every AWeber receipt, form or order surface.

GetResponse Creator documents an included 100-attendee hosted room and an integrated course builder, with US lower-tier add-on availability still unconfirmed. AWeber’s inspected official route shows external webinar tools and a Zoom registration route through Zapier; that inspected route does not prove product-wide absence of a native host, but the external host and connector fees are unpriced without a quote. If you need a verified included route, **GetResponse Creator is the supported choice**. An existing AWeber customer should obtain a current quote for the external route before assuming total cost. See the <a href="https://www.getresponse.com/help/what-are-getresponse-webinars.html" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">GetResponse webinar page</a> and <a href="https://www.aweber.com/integrations/webinar/" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">AWeber webinar integrations page</a>.

**Compare support channels and hours.** AWeber lists phone support Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–8 p.m. Eastern and email/chat channels. GetResponse lists email and chat on self-service plans and phone support only on Enterprise. A phone line is a real edge for buyers who want voice contact. It says nothing about response time or agent knowledge. See the [AWeber business-hours page](https://docs.aweber.com/getting-started-with-aweber/aweber-information/what-are-aweber-s-business-hours) and <a href="https://www.getresponse.com/help/getresponse-pricing-plans.html" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">GetResponse plan page</a>.

Before paying extra for an all-in-one bundle, inventory what you already own. If you already pay for a site builder, funnel tool, course host or webinar platform, GetResponse’s broader Creator package may duplicate those tools or push you into a higher tier to unlock one missing feature. That is an operating-cost question, not a statement about feature quality.

## Billing states and send caps: where the invoice forms

**GetResponse’s current billing help and US terms count per-list copies and a current-month set of stored, added, unsubscribed, deleted, rejected, unconfirmed and inactive records.** A contact in two lists counts twice, and a contact removed this month may still affect that month’s count. Check the <a href="https://www.getresponse.com/help/how-does-getresponse-pricing-work.html" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">GetResponse billing page</a> and <a href="https://www.getresponse.com/legal/terms-us" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">US terms</a> before assuming the plan price equals your bill.

**AWeber explicitly excludes unsubscribed records from billing**, according to its current help. AWeber’s documentation also says a reduced count does not automatically downgrade your tier; **you request a change through support**. Do not delete suppression or consent history just to lower a count. The pages we inspected do not resolve whether duplicate addresses across multiple lists or unconfirmed records share that billing treatment; verify that directly before assuming the invoice. See the [AWeber unsubscribe help](https://docs.aweber.com/subscriber-management/subscribers/how-do-i-delete-my-unsubscribes), [downgrade help](https://docs.aweber.com/account-management/account-management/does-deleting-subscribers-automatically-adjust-my-) and [deduplication help](https://docs.aweber.com/subscriber-management/subscribers/how-do-i-deduplicate-my-lists).

**GetResponse paid plans include unlimited sends under their terms**, but the list-extension fee can appear monthly even on an annual plan if billable contacts exceed the purchased band. “Unlimited sends” is not “unlimited free contact capacity.”

**AWeber paid plans cap sends at 10× the purchased subscriber-tier capacity on Lite and 12× on Plus, then automatically move the account to the next subscriber tier.** The cap is measured against the tier’s maximum contact band, not your exact active count. A 1,000-contact Lite plan has a 10,000-send allowance whether your actual list is 700 or 1,000. A 30,000-message month at that band would exceed the allowance; AWeber is not unable to send more, but you pay for the larger tier. Daily broadcasts plus welcome and re-engagement mail approach the cap faster. Price the next tier when your send pattern threatens it. See the [AWeber send-limits help](https://docs.aweber.com/getting-started-with-aweber/aweber-information/how-many-messages-can-i-send-per-day).

## Migrating between platforms without losing your list

Both vendors document migration services, but neither result was tested here. **AWeber advertises free expert migration** covering lists, tags, subscribers, up to 3 templates, forms and pages, and automations; account eligibility and exact mapping must be confirmed. **GetResponse offers custom migration** after a survey or discovery call, and we could not quote a total. See the <a href="https://www.aweber.com/migration.htm" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">AWeber migration page</a> and <a href="https://www.getresponse.com/move-to-getresponse" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">GetResponse migration page</a>.

Before closing an old account, run the checks that reduce silent failures: export a sample CSV and inspect how custom fields and statuses map; preserve suppression and consent records; avoid triggering a second welcome email during import; validate forms, payments and any external webinar route; and compare the final quote to your own billable-count estimate. A working incumbent platform is not automatically wrong. **Stay put when the current system still meets your needs and the saved cost or required capability does not outweigh rebuilding, testing and retraining.** Our [email platform migration guide](/moving-email-platforms) walks through the full sequence.

## Which buyer should pick which platform

**Newsletter and low-volume senders.** At 500 paid contacts, **AWeber Lite at $15 per month** is lower. At 1,000 contacts with one welcome sequence and weekly broadcasts, **GetResponse Starter at $19 per month** costs less and includes more send headroom and unlimited pages. If paid phone support and broadcast A/B testing matter more, AWeber Lite may justify the higher price.

**Behavioral-workflow and A/B-testing buyers.** **AWeber Plus at $45 per month** and **GetResponse Marketer at $59 per month** are documented paid routes to compare. Marketer is also the required tier when tagging, scoring, more-than-six-element workflows or workflow quantity rules Starter out. GetResponse Marketer adds native percentage splitting; AWeber Plus keeps phone support. Lite is not the behavioral-workflow match.

**Creators requiring a verified hosted webinar or course route.** **GetResponse Creator from $69 per month at 1,000 contacts** is the documented included route. If you are on AWeber, quote the external host and connector before assuming the total cost is lower.

**Current customers considering a switch.** Switch for a concrete saved cost or a required capability the current platform cannot provide, not because a comparison page changed. If the incumbent still does the job, staying put is a valid ending. If neither set of eligible plans supports your actual rule, integration or budget, expand the shortlist: read our [ActiveCampaign review](/activecampaign-review) for a different automation-focused product, or the [best email marketing platforms page](/best-email-marketing-software).
